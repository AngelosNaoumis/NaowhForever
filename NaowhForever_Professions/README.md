# Professions

Naowh's profession window in place of the game's: your recipes by category with the ones you
have not learned below them, the chosen recipe in the middle (reagents, profit, craft buttons, or
where to learn it), Blizzard's own profession tabs on its edge, and an overview card for each
profession. Around it: craft orders from another player's link, your alts' professions, buying
reagents on the auction house or at a merchant, a shopping list, your favourite recipes at the
trainer and the auction house, a next-rank alert, a batch craft timer, a tracking reminder and your
enchants beside the trade window. Laid out like the
[Dungeon Journal](../NaowhForever_DungeonJournal/README.md), on [`Shared/`](../Shared/README.md).

## Why

- Minimap Node Tooltips only adds a line to the game's own minimap tooltip (`TooltipDataProcessor`,
  `MinimapMouseover`), which the minimap redraws every frame while hovered, so the skills it compares
  are read on `SKILL_LINES_CHANGED` instead. It was Minimap Node Icons, which drew each node's item over
  its tracking dot. No API says where those dots are, so it found them by moving the minimap's soft cursor
  (`Minimap:SetUsingSoftCursor`, `Minimap:UpdateMouseoverAtPoint`) over a grid and asking
  `C_TooltipInfo.GetMinimapMouseover` what was under each point. A Forever update in November 2026 stopped
  that: the probe now names a node only under the real cursor (#918), so the pictures, their Icon Size and
  Glimmer's hand-off to them were removed. The switch keeps its saved key, `nodeIcons`.
- The minimap tooltip names a node in the client's language and the locale files have no node names, so
  `Data/GatherNodes.lua` is keyed by the English name and Minimap Node Tooltips works only on an English
  client (`enUS`, `enGB`). On any other its card is not shown and it stays off, even with a profile that
  has it on, so it never looks on while doing nothing.
- `Data/GatherNodes.lua` repeats Glimmer's herb and vein names and skills (`Tools/build/glimmer_nodes.py`,
  from Wowhead Forever) instead of reading `ns.Glimmer.NODES`: Glimmer is in Quality of Life, which
  Professions does not depend on, and its table is keyed by object ID while the minimap gives only a
  name. `test-node-tooltip.lua` checks the two match. Each item ID is the game's own `ItemSparse` row of
  that name (Copper Ore for Copper Vein). Chunk of Amber has no item there, so New Herbs and Ores shows
  the Find Minerals icon for it.
- The skill compared is the profession's rank plus its `skillModifier`: gear, enchant and racial bonuses
  count toward a node's requirement, and the game's own rank bar shows them as part of the skill.
  Glimmer compares the same. The tooltip line is red below the node's skill, then orange, yellow, green
  and grey at `SKILL_ORANGE`, `SKILL_YELLOW` and `SKILL_GREEN` (25, 50 and 100) over it, as Glimmer
  colours it. These and `FIND_SPELL` are in `Constants.lua`; Glimmer's own are in QoL, out of reach.
- New Herbs and Ores compares Herbalism and Mining (rank plus bonus, as gathering checks them) with the
  highest each has been since login, on `SKILL_LINES_CHANGED`, and names every node of
  `Data/GatherNodes.lua` whose skill lies between the two (`NodeScan.Unlocked`). Nothing shows at login
  or when a profession is learned (from 0), and a bonus taken off and put on again does not show the
  same nodes twice. Unlearning a profession forgets its highest, so learning it again shows its nodes anew
  as it climbs. One icon per item, so Silver Vein and its Ooze Covered twin share Silver Ore's. It
  is the shared toast (`Parts.Toast`) and goes after 20 seconds, as the Level-Up Toast does. It names nodes
  as the minimap tooltip does, in English, so it too is offered only on an English client.
- Skill-up Thresholds is off by default. It creates its line only when an enabled recipe pane first
  needs it. Selection and skill updates use the existing redraw path; this feature adds no events,
  hooks, timers, route planning or polling.
- Each recipe's `yellow` and `grey` in `RecipeData.lua` are written by `Tools/build/skill_thresholds.py`
  from the game's own `SkillLineAbility` table (`TrivialSkillLineRankLow` and `TrivialSkillLineRankHigh`),
  through `Tools/sources/wago.py` with the build's hotfixes. They sit on the recipe entries that already
  exist rather than in a table of their own, which cost about 165 KB at login and 415 KB more once used;
  on the entries they add about 55 KB. Rerun the tool when `wago.BUILD` changes or recipes are added: it
  rewrites only those two fields. Only crafting skill lines and recipe categories are read;
  contradictory ranges and conflicting duplicate spell rows are left out. A yellow equal to its grey is
  kept: such a recipe (Forever's 35 to 40 recipes, some smelting) is orange until it turns grey.
- Orange starts at the skill the recipe is learned at (`skill`), not the table's `MinSkillLineRank`,
  which is 1 for nearly every recipe. A recipe learned past its yellow is yellow from the start, so
  yellow and green are at least the learn skill. Green is the rounded-down midpoint of yellow and grey.
  A positive live `maxTrivialLevel` replaces the bundled grey, and green is recalculated with it.
  A grey below yellow, or at or below the learn skill, draws no line; a grey equal to yellow draws
  orange straight into grey. The marker's percentage estimates skill-up chance as
  `(grey - skill) / (grey - yellow)`, clamped to 0-100% and rounded to a whole percent, with the data's own
  yellow: a recipe learned past its yellow is under 100% from the start, as its yellow band says. This is a
  formula-based estimate, not a measured probability. The recipe name's colour still comes from the game's
  `relativeDifficulty`.
- The line is drawn below the icon, with the description and reagents beneath it, for an unlearned recipe
  too: what it would give if learned now. Its current-skill marker adds the profession's `skillModifier`;
  below the learn skill it has no percentage. Linked professions have no marker or range: their
  recipes belong to someone else, not the current character.
- Skill-up Chances is off by default and adds no events, hooks or timers: the list reads the chance as it
  draws its visible rows, with the skill read once a draw. A learned recipe the game colours orange is
  100% and grey 0%, whatever the data says, so the number never disagrees with the name; yellow and green
  use the formula above. An unlearned recipe has a chance only under Learnable now. Each percentage's text
  is made once and reused. Recipes are found in `RecipeData` through an index of only the professions
  opened, built on first use.
- Best for Skill-ups lists your recipes in one group, without categories, and leaves out those with no
  chance. It orders them by cost per skill-up: the reagents, at the cheaper of a merchant and the last
  price scan and leaving out the ones you marked as had, less what the result sells to a vendor for,
  divided by the chance. Vendoring is what a levelling craft is sure to fetch; the auction house price is
  left out because a craft made to level is often not worth listing. The Skill-up Plan, which uses the
  same cost, says so under its steps, and a recipe's Crafting Profit tooltip shows the vendor sale under
  the auction one: a craft that vendors for more than its materials but lists for less read as a gain in
  the plan and a loss on the recipe, with nothing saying why. A recipe with an unpriced reagent, or
  a result whose sell price has not loaded yet, comes after the priced ones, by chance. The cost is cached
  per recipe with the profit, until the next full draw. The unlearned groups keep their own order.
- Plan by Best Sale Price (off by default) changes only the Skill-up Plan's cost: the result counts at the
  best of its vendor sale, its auction price after the cut and its disenchant value after the cut. Best for
  Skill-ups and the per skill-up line keep the vendor sale, so a setting found under the plan does not
  move the recipe list. The plan reads it once per plan and keeps it in the plan's cache key, and
  `Prices.NetCost` keeps a second cache for it, so turning it on or off plans again at once.
- The auction price counts only when at least `LISTED_ENOUGH` (10) auctions listed the item in the same
  scan or release (`ns.AuctionListed`), a stack counting once: one seller's stack at a silly price would
  otherwise pick every step, and the plan crafts dozens of each. A price with no count, from a scan or
  release made before counts were kept, counts as too few.
- The disenchant value is the same estimate as Disenchant Deals' (`DisenchantValues.Expected` on the
  last prices, at the item's own level from `GetDetailedItemLevelInfo`), and counts only when the
  character's Enchanting meets the item's requirement, as The Market's Best Way to Sell does. The plan
  reads the Enchanting skill once per plan and keeps it in its cache key, and `Prices.NetCost` drops its
  best-sale cache when the skill it was worked out at changes, so learning Enchanting plans again.
- The disenchant value prices the enchanting materials from the last prices without a count check, so
  a thin market for a shard or essence can still lift it. Checking those counts would need a counted
  price list through `DisenchantValues.Expected`; not done yet.
- Skill-up Plan is off by default; its title bar button and its pane are made the first time they are
  needed, and its only hook is `Window.Render`, which places the button (as Disenchant does). Opening it
  shows the plan in the middle column in place of the recipe; clicking a recipe in the list, or a step,
  goes back to the recipe.
- The plan walks from your skill to your cap a point at a time and takes, at each point, the craft with
  the lowest cost per skill-up (as Best for Skill-ups ranks them), using your recipes and the trainer
  recipes under Learnable now. It keeps the craft it is on until another is more than `STICKY` (10%)
  cheaper a point, so two near-equal recipes do not take turns. Without prices, the likeliest craft wins.
  A point's expected crafts are one over its chance; a step's crafts are their sum, rounded up.
- A trainer recipe stays in the plan only if the plan without it costs more, its training included,
  or reaches less far. The plan is tried again without each one it uses until none can go; a recipe's
  training is counted once. Recipes on a cooldown are never planned. The plan stops early, and says so,
  where nothing you know or can train gives a skill-up.
- The trainer comparisons reuse one scratch plan and its steps within each calculation. The winning
  plan stays separate, so trying more alternatives does not allocate a new set of tables each time.
- The plan is worked out when the pane opens and again only when the skill, the cap, the recipes, the
  last price scan or Plan Recipes You Can Train Later changes, not on every draw: it is a few thousand
  chance sums. Queue Plan queues each step through the Crafting Queue (with its prerequisites and
  missing materials); without the queue, Add Plan to List puts each step on the Shopping List. Both wait
  until every Train step is learned. The disabled button keeps its tooltip
  (`SetMotionScriptsWhileDisabled`) to say why.
- Plan Recipes You Can Train Later is off by default and adds no events, hooks or frames. On, the plan
  also uses the trainer recipes under Needs more skill, each only from the skill its trainer asks for
  (`ns.RecipeFinder.Skill`, which takes the trainer's own number once you have visited one); its step
  reads Train at that skill. Trainers check your rank without its bonus, so this does too, while the
  skill-up chance still counts the bonus. Recipes under Needs next rank stay out, as the plan ends at
  your cap. Trainer recipes have no level requirement in `RecipeData.lua`, so none is checked. It needs
  Unlearned Recipes, which finds the trainer recipes.
- With it on, Queue Plan and Add Plan to List take only the steps before the first Train at step, since
  the steps after it start where that recipe leaves off, and still wait while one of those is a Train
  step. Their tooltip says up to which skill.
- It makes the plan dearer to work out, since many more trainer recipes are tried in and out of it:
  about 9 ms at worst offline (Leatherworking from 125 to 225 with no recipe past skill 1 learned),
  against under 1 ms off. It is still worked out only on the triggers above.
- The on/off switches' defaults (`enabled` and each feature's switch) are read from
  `ns.FEATURES.professions`; their options keep their own defaults in `Professions.lua`.
- Blizzard's window stays open under ours at zero alpha: hiding it would close the trade skill (its
  OnHide calls CloseTradeSkill) and take the recipe data with it. Ours sits one strata higher, so
  nothing of Blizzard's can be clicked, and is never narrower or shorter than it.
- It goes to zero alpha in its own OnShow, before it is drawn: ours takes over on the next frame, and
  Blizzard's window, put back to full alpha whenever it closes, showed for that frame on every open.
- Under Forever's Gamepad interface style (`ns.GamepadStandDown()`) the recipe window, Train Favorites
  and Favorite Recipe Marks stay off and the game's own windows are used. That style drives the
  profession, trainer and merchant windows with the D-pad from secure code, which the invisible
  window under ours, the docked tabs and cards, and our hooks on those windows would taint.
- Blizzard's profession tabs switch profession by casting its spell, which only Blizzard's code may
  do, so the real tabs move onto our window's right edge, or its left edge with Dock Shopping List on,
  and ignore their invisible parent's alpha.
- On the left edge the tabs are mirrored so their open side faces the window, and put back when
  undocked: their atlas art is flipped and their anchors swapped; the icons keep their look. The
  icon's mask is turned half a turn instead: a MaskTexture with flipped tex coords hides the icon,
  and the side tab mask is the same top and bottom, so turning it mirrors it. Clicking a tab
  re-anchors every icon to its right-side spot, so a SetPoint hook mirrors it again.
- What hangs off the window is kept on screen: the profession tabs, on either edge, and the docked
  Shopping List. On opening, when it widens, on letting go of a drag and when the Shopping List docks,
  the window (and Blizzard's under it) moves just enough to bring them back, the left edge first when
  they cannot all fit. Otherwise it may still be dragged mostly off screen, as our windows may
  (`ns.AllowOffscreen`); a docked Shopping List is as tall as the window, so with it the whole window
  stays on screen. The game opens it at the screen's left edge, where tabs on the left would be off.
- It is fit only with the tabs docked on it, so not while it is still opening (a docked Shopping List
  reopens in its OnShow, before it is moved to its place), and not while a move waits for combat to
  end, which would save the spot it has not left yet; after combat it is moved, then fit. Less than
  `FIT_SLACK` (a pixel) past an edge, left over from converting scales, does not count.
- Docked, the Shopping List is not clamped to the screen, so it stays beside the window and is measured
  where it is: clamped, the screen held it back over the window.
- Dock Shopping List (`shoppingListDock`) is off by default. Off, the Shopping List is a window of its
  own and the tabs stay on the right edge. It is read once,
  when the profession window and the Shopping List are first built, so changing it asks for a reload.
- With it on, the Shopping List docks on the right edge, where the tabs used to be. Opened from the
  profession window it docks; Detach floats it where it is, to drag and resize, and Dock puts it back.
  Closing it ends the detach. It comes back with the profession window only if it was docked and open
  when that window closed (account `shoppingListPanel`).
- Reagents (`reagentUses`) is off by default and needs the Shopping List: the recipes each character
  knows are the ones the Shopping List saves when a profession window opens (`profMakes`), as The
  Market's Crafts reads them. A recipe is yours when the game says it is learned or your saved recipes
  have it, and an alt's when another character on your realm (the realm in its GUID) saved it.
- The game gives reagents only for the profession window that is open, so every recipe's reagents
  come from `Data/RecipeReagents.lua`, written by `Tools/build/recipe_reagents.py` from the game's own
  SpellReagents table, with what a craft makes from SpellEffect (0 there means one). Its index, every
  reagent's recipes, is built the first time the list draws, not at load.
- Reagents prices as Crafts does: a craft sells for its auction price after the 5% cut, times what it
  makes, and gains that less what its materials sell for as they are (the auction price after the cut
  or the vendor sale, whichever is more; a vendor reagent at what it costs). A reagent's row says Craft
  only for the recipe you or an alt know that gains most for each of it used, and says nothing when no
  recipe beats selling: its tooltip already has its prices. One you would first have to learn is listed
  but never named on the row.
- Where to learn a recipe is Recipe Finder's own text (`Describe`), handed the recipe's profession and
  your skill in it instead of the open window's. For a profession you do not have, its cap is taken as
  unlimited so no next-rank step shows. Clicking the recipe sets a waypoint to the first place in that
  text that is on a map, which is the nearest trainer, seller or quest giver.
- The title-bar buttons (Disenchant, Shopping List, Reagents, Crafting Queue, Skill-up Plan, Alts, Link) are lined up by `UI/TitleButtons.lua`
  rather than each placing itself: each shown one takes the next place left of the close button, in a
  set order, so any mix of features never overlaps and a hidden button leaves no gap. Each re-lines
  them when it shows or hides, so the features need not know about each other.
- The overview's spell buttons (Smelting, Find Minerals) cast and the red cross unlearns, so they stay
  Blizzard's. A card draws its buttons as part of itself, so Blizzard's whole card moves into ours with
  its art at zero alpha; the buttons stay children of that card, where their click reads the
  spellbook offset. They are secure: docked out of combat only, and undocked whenever the overview
  is not showing.
- Blizzard only makes a tab for a profession you have learned, so an unlearned one has none. The docked
  tabs on the right edge get a Naowh-blue outline so they stand out on the dark window; mirrored onto
  the left edge they read as part of it and have none. The outline is the tab's own hover art
  (`common-sidetab-hover`, which Forever swaps for its `-c60` art as it does for Blizzard's tab) tinted
  blue, so it follows the bevelled corners; a square border round the tab frame sat off the art, which
  is taller than the frame and has empty space on its outer side. It is drawn under Blizzard's gold
  selected art so the open tab still shows. The Unlearn button beside a skill bar is drawn from the
  atlas `Profession-button-red-crossmark` at 20px and full strength; where that atlas is missing it falls back to the red cross of `ReadyCheck-NotReady`, or the button shows nothing.
- With the overview docked our window holds secure buttons and is protected, so closing it in combat
  makes it transparent instead of hiding it. Resizing waits for combat to end for the same reason.
  It cannot be dragged in combat either, and a drag still going when combat starts ends there:
  `PLAYER_REGEN_DISABLED` fires before the lockdown, while it can still be let go of.
- Once dragged, Blizzard's window is moved to where ours was put, out of combat only (it holds secure
  buttons); anchoring it to ours instead would make ours protected. The panel manager places it
  again whenever any panel opens or closes, so its SetPoint is hooked. EllesmereUI's Shifter puts
  ProfessionsFrame back at its own saved spot on every SetPoint, so with the Shifter on that saved
  spot is written too.
- A trade link reads `trade:<crafter GUID>:<spell>:<skill line>`; only a plain click opens it. Opened
  from a closed window, a link does not always read as linked yet, so the click is remembered. A
  link clicked while Blizzard's window is loaded but closed makes Forever cast every one of your
  professions and land on the last, so it is clicked once more after `RETRY_AFTER` (0.5s). The view
  ends when the window closes, but not on the close the click itself causes (`SETTLE`, 1s), or when
  you cast one of your own professions. Mining's window opens with Smelting (2656), not a spell
  named after the profession.
- Your Alts' Professions is off by default. On, a character opening its own profession (not a linked,
  guild or NPC list, and only while `CanTradeSkillListLink` allows it) saves that profession's
  `GetTradeSkillListLink` account-wide in `profLinks`, by GUID, with its name, class, realm and faction.
  Neither call is in the generated API docs; Blizzard's own Forever profession window calls both for its
  Link button. Every list update while its own window is open saves the link again when it changed: the

  window; it comes back when combat ends.
- What an enchant goes on is the game's own SpellEquippedItems row for its recipe (`Data/EnchantSlots.lua`):
  the item class, then the subclass mask and the inventory type mask, each 0 for any.
  `C_TradeSkillUI.GetEnchantItems` and `IsEnchantTargetValid` answer only for items in your bags.
- The rows are your learned enchants that fit, the ones asked for first, then by the skill they turn grey
  at (`maxTrivialLevel`), highest first; one whose reagents you lack is greyed. Eight show
  (`SIDE_PANEL_ROWS`) and the mouse wheel scrolls the rest.
- A whisper, party or instance chat line that links an enchant recipe (`|Henchant:<recipe>|h`, the link
  a Craft Orders message sends for an enchant) marks that enchant as asked for by its sender, by GUID. The
  marks stay through a cancelled or reopened trade, so the request is still there when the enchant is
  done; all of them go once 20 players have some (`MAX_ASKERS`), so the list stays small. A secret line
  or sender is not read.
- Under the Gamepad interface style it stays off, as the recipe window and Train Favorites do: that style
  moves through the game's windows from secure code, and the panel has not been tried with it.
- Disenchant Deals is a separate opt-in feature. It reads the immutable lowest-buyout map from the
  last manual Scan Prices; it never starts or repeats an auction scan. The material bands, quantities
  and odds are the classic-era facts from Warcraft Wiki's
  [Disenchanting tables](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Disenchanting_tables)
  ([CC BY-SA 4.0](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Warcraft_Wiki:Copyrights)), represented
  in original compact rows for Uncommon, Rare and Epic weapons and armor. A deal is omitted when any
  possible material has no scan price, instead of overstating an incomplete expected value.
- Auctionable equipment marked `ITEM_FLAG_NO_DISENCHANT` (`ItemSparse.Flags_0` bit `0x8000`) in
  Forever build 1.60.1.70450 is omitted from the Disenchant window and both deal modes, and receives
  no detailed disenchant tooltip. This covers the crafted/event wands, Twilight Cultist set pieces
  and Spring Circlet that otherwise resemble eligible Uncommon equipment.
- Profit mode ranks positive expected returns after the 5% auction cut by profit and ROI. It includes
  every skill level unless For my Enchant Lvl is checked. Guaranteed profit means even the least
  valuable possible result is worth more than the item. Level Up ranks items the character can disenchant by
  low cash investment first, keeps only ten visible choices, and shows none at skill 60 or above.
- Level Up's skill-up bands come from Forever build 1.60.1.70425's `SkillLineAbility` row for
  Disenchant (13262), read through [wago.tools](https://wago.tools/db2/SkillLineAbility): minimum 1,
  low trivial 20 and high trivial 60. Those thresholds map to guaranteed below 20, likely from 20 to
  39, unlikely from 40 to 59 and no skill-up at 60; they do not vary with the item being disenchanted.
- Calculations run only after Find Deals, in bounded chunks. Item data loads five at a time; closing
  the auction house or changing modes pauses at the saved cursor, and the completed results stay
  cached until Scan Prices changes. Crossing a disenchant skill bracket invalidates only Level Up.
  The outcome arrays are shared by class, quality and item level instead of allocated per item.
- Buy searches only the chosen item. Each click buys one current cheapest listing, then rechecks that
  item, recolours a price increase and reinserts only that row at its new rank. The window registers
  search, purchase and skill events only while the auction house is open; when disabled it creates no
  frame, event, hook or timer. Detailed outcome tooltips are another opt-in setting.
  Their Enchanting requirement line is green when the character meets it, and red when the
  character has no Enchanting profession or has less than the required skill.
- Only When Worth Disenchanting shows the detailed tooltip only when the expected value after the cut
  beats the item's vendor price and, when it has one, its scan price after the cut
  (`Deals.WorthDisenchanting`): an item better sold gets no breakdown. With no scan price only the
  vendor is weighed; an unpriced disenchant never shows. It is worked out from Professions' own entry,
  not The Market's verdict, which Professions could read only while The Market is loaded. Holding Shift
  shows it anyway; the game's bag buttons rebuild their tooltip while hovered (`UpdateTooltip`), so
  pressing Shift there needs no new hover.
